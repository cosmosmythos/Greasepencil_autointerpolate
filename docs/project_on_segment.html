Mockup: only 3 strokes — stroke 0, 1, 2

Real Shirt has 40 strokes & 17-segment lines — that's why it looked cramped. This mockup uses 3 clean strokes with 2–3 segments each, spaced apart, so you can see what distance(0.end → stroke 1) actually measures.

What "stroke 1" is

not one point — it's a polyline

Reading it: Each stroke is a line with a middle joint (white dot). stroke 1 — This means: the whole purple polyline from a(top) → mid → b(bottom), not a single coordinate. When we say distance(0.end → stroke 1) we mean to anywhere on that purple line.

One segment: the slider t

just a knob 0→1. Before / inside / past.

t 0.50 → clamped 0.50
what t = ap·ab / len² meansshadow of ap onto ab
No math magic: ap is arrow a→p, ab is a→b. Dot tells how much ap points the same way as ab (its shadow length) ÷ ab's length. That's the knob.
why clamp[0,1] keeps you on the segment
-0.3 → 0 snaps to a. 1.35 → 1 snaps to b. Without it you'd land off the stroke, floating in air.
Plain: a= start of segment, b= end, p= your point, closest = slide t along a→b, drop straight down to p. Paper calls this "projection point" (docs/Yang18-SCA.md:66) — This means: same slider, paper just says it in words.
